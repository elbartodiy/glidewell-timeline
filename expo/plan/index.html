Looking down on the hall. x runs left to right along the rail the camera travels; z runs up the sheet, away from you — the wall is at the top of each plate and the pedestals are at the bottom, nearest the lens. Every layer is placed at the depth the renderer actually gives it, because depth is the single number that decides whether a thing sits behind the counter, on it, or in front of it. Nothing here is drawn from memory: the numbers come out of the file itself.

Sheet S1 · Section through any bay

Every layer in the hall, listed by depth, nearest the camera at the bottom. The ruler on the left holds the true spacing — twenty-six depths gather into two knots, one at the counter and one at the wall, with four hundred units of empty room between them. The rows are evenly spaced and joined to the ruler by leaders, because at true height the labels print through each other.

Sheet P1 · Plan, four plates

One plate per era, each at its own horizontal scale, depth at one scale throughout so the plates can be compared. The exaggeration is printed on each.
